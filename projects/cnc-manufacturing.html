CNC Manufacturing

Programming and operating the 3-axis Tormach CNC mill to produce precision ARES parts. CAM setup in Fusion 360 through to finished aluminium 6061 parts.

Overview

As part of my role at ARES, I handle a portion of the manufacturing pipeline for machined components — creating CAM toolpaths in Fusion 360, setting up workholding, stock procurement, operating the Tormach 1100MX, and maintaining tolerances specified in engineering drawings.

For components with off-axis geometries, I have designed 3D-printable soft jaws to remove the need for higher-axis milling

MachineTormach 1100MX — 3-axis CNC mill
CAM softwareFusion 360
Materials6061 Aluminium
ComponentsRecovery side hatch reinforcement struts, spring piston mounts, structural hatch tabs
WorkholdingStandard vice + 3D-printed custom soft jaws

CAM Programming

Each component starts with a Fusion 360 CAM setup — selecting operations, tools, feeds and speeds, and simulating the full toolpath before sending G-code to the machine.

Left: Fusion 360 CAM setup for recovery strut. Right: Spring piston mount

Machining

Parts are machined on the university's Tormach 1100MX with flood coolant for 6061 aluminium. Fixturing and datum setup are done manually.

Left: Tormach 1100MX during a machining run.

Finished Components

Machined parts are deburred, inspected, and checked against drawing tolerances before being handed off to the relevant sub-team.

Finished 6061 aluminium recovery side hatch reinforcement struts.
