Paraffin Fuel Grain Spin-Cast System

Designed and fabricated a spin-casting rig to manufacture paraffin fuel grains with consistent cylindrical port geometry — enabling repeatable, void-free hybrid motor propellant production in-house.

Problem

Paraffin wax is an attractive hybrid fuel due to its high regression rate, low cost, and ease of handling. Gravity-cast methods produce air bubbles and internal voids that cause unstable burn characteristics. Spin-cast fuel grains offer a monolithic, void-free structure with a concentric port.

The Rig

The rig spins a cylindrical mould about its central axis while the paraffin solidifies. Centrifugal force drives the melt outward uniformly, leaving a clean concentric port along the axis.

Left: rig during assembly. Right: operational setup — Arduino PWM via ESC, bench power supply, acrylic containment shield.

Key design decisions:

  • Motor and belt-drive sized to test variable RPM — optimal spin speed determined experimentally for the target grain geometry
  • Mounted on a T-slot extrusion so grain length can be changed without redesigning the rig
  • ESC controls motor speed via Arduino PWM
  • Alignment features machined into mould end-caps to ensure coaxiality of the port mandrel
  • Insulated enclosure to slow cooling rate and reduce residual stress

Casting Process

The procedure was developed iteratively through test casts. Each grain was sectioned and inspected for voids, port concentricity, and surface finish. Chemical safety and hazard operability studies were completed and submitted before any spin casting commenced.

Left: cast paraffin grain showing the concentric port produced by spin casting. Right: dimensional inspection — outer diameter measured at 57.12 mm.

Gyroid Casting Development

I conducted a parallel development investigating casting paraffin into ABS 3D-printed gyroid structures. The gyroid geometry increases the strength of the fuel. Since Paraffin is notorious for cracking during ignition, the ABS gyroid offers significantly better strength through the grain whilst maintaining a high regression rate. The ABS gyroid is printed, filled with molten paraffin, then used as an alternative fuel grain.
