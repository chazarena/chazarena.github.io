Paraffin Fuel Grain Spin-Cast System

Designed and fabricated a spin-casting rig to manufacture paraffin fuel grains with consistent cylindrical port geometry — enabling repeatable, void-free hybrid motor propellant production in-house.

Problem

Paraffin wax is an attractive hybrid fuel because of its high regression rate, low cost, and ease of handling. But casting a paraffin grain with a concentric, smooth port is harder than it sounds — gravity-cast methods produce eccentric ports and surface voids that cause erratic burn profiles and reduce motor predictability.

ARES needed a repeatable in-house manufacturing process that could produce grains to the tolerances required by the motor sizing model, without access to industrial casting equipment.

Spin-cast rig — full assembly photo

Design

The rig spins a cylindrical mould about its central axis while the paraffin solidifies. Centrifugal force drives the melt outward, pressing it uniformly against the mould wall and leaving a clean, concentric port along the axis. Key design decisions:

  • Motor and belt-drive selected to achieve the minimum RPM required to overcome gravity-induced eccentricity for the paraffin density and grain geometry
  • Mould end-caps designed with a controlled pour aperture to minimise turbulence during filling
  • Alignment features machined into the mould to ensure coaxiality of the port mandrel
  • Insulated enclosure to slow cooling rate and reduce residual stress in the grain
  • Quick-release locking system for safe mould removal after solidification

Casting Process

The casting procedure was developed iteratively — each grain was inspected by sectioning a test sample to check for voids, port concentricity, and surface finish before the process was locked in for production.

  • Paraffin melted to controlled temperature to achieve correct viscosity before pour
  • Mould pre-heated to reduce thermal shock and surface delamination
  • Rig brought to operating speed before pour to ensure immediate centrifugal action
  • Cooling rate managed to avoid cracking from differential thermal contraction
  • Final grain inspected dimensionally and visually before acceptance
