Paraffin Fuel Grain Spin-Cast System

Designed and fabricated a spin-casting rig to manufacture paraffin fuel grains with consistent cylindrical port geometry — enabling repeatable, void-free hybrid motor propellant production in-house.

Problem

Paraffin wax is an attractive hybrid fuel because of its high regression rate, low cost, and ease of handling. But casting a paraffin grain with a concentric, smooth port is harder than it sounds. Gravity-cast methods produce air bubbles and internal voids that cause unstable burn characteristics. Spin-cast fuel grains offer a monolithic structure.

ARES needed a repeatable in-house manufacturing process that could produce grains to the tolerances required by the motor sizing model, without access to industrial casting equipment.

Spin-cast rig — full assembly photo

Design

The rig spins a cylindrical mould about its central axis while the paraffin solidifies. Centrifugal force drives the paraffin outward, pressing it uniformly against the mould wall and leaving a clean, concentric port along the axis. Key design decisions:

  • Motor and belt-drive selected so various RMPs could be tested to determine the optimal settings for the fuel grain we desire
  • Alignment features machined into the mould to ensure coaxiality of the port mandrel
  • Insulated enclosure to slow cooling rate and reduce residual stress in the grain
  • System mounted on t-slot extrusion so the length of fuel grain required could be changed
  • ESC controls motor via programmed Arduino (PWM)

Casting Process

The casting procedure was developed iteratively — each grain was inspected by sectioning a test sample to check for voids, port concentricity, and surface finish before the process was locked in for production. Chemical safety and hazard operability studies were completed and submitted before any spin casting.
