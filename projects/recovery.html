Recovery & Parachute Systems

Built and tested dual-deployment parachute systems to bring ARES vehicles back safely — from drogue deployment at apogee through main deployment at low altitude.

Overview

Recovery is the most safety-critical system on a high-powered rocket: a failure means an uncontrolled vehicle returning to ground at terminal velocity. Competition rules specify maximum landing velocity and require a fully independent dual-deployment system with electronic redundancy at every stage.

My work on the recovery sub-team covered system design, parachute selection and sizing, ejection charge testing, and the milestone review documentation required by competition rules.

System Design

The dual-deployment architecture uses a small drogue parachute to stabilise the descending vehicle immediately after apogee, and a larger main parachute deployed at low altitude (typically 300–500 m) to bring the vehicle to a safe landing speed.

  • Drogue and main parachute sized from descent velocity targets and vehicle mass budget
  • Black powder ejection charges sized by empirical testing across a range of charge masses
  • Shear pins selected to withstand max dynamic pressure during ascent while shearing reliably on ejection
  • Dual altimeter bays with independent power and independent deployment channels
  • Shock cord and harness rated to deployment loads with appropriate safety factor

Testing & Validation

Each system was validated through a structured ground-test campaign before any flight attempt. Ejection charges were characterised at bench level, followed by full-system deployment tests in the recovery bay to verify parachute packing, harness routing, and separation behaviour.

  • Black powder charge sizing verified empirically: multiple charge masses tested to confirm reliable separation across expected temperature range
  • Shock cord deployment tests to verify packing configuration doesn't cause entanglement
  • Altimeter function tests: continuity checks, arming sequences, and simulated deployment events
  • Full system assembly review against the CDR checklist before launch approval

Design Reviews & Documentation

Completed LDR (Launch Day Readiness), PCR (Pre-Competition Review), and CDR (Critical Design Review) for the recovery system. Co-authored the technical report submitted to competition judges, covering system architecture, design rationale, safety analysis, and test results.

Also delivered a technical presentation on recovery system design at the Victorian Rocketry Association, covering dual-deployment best practice and lessons learned.
